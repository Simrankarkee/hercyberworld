Children go online younger every year, for school, games and videos. Blocking everything rarely works. The goal is to build **safe habits** and an open relationship where kids tell you when something feels wrong.

## Set up the basics

- **Google Family Link** (Android) or **Screen Time** (iPhone/iPad) to manage apps, purchases and screen time
- **YouTube Kids** instead of regular YouTube for younger children
- Turn on **SafeSearch** in Google
- Respect age limits: most social media requires users to be at least 13
- Keep devices in shared family spaces for younger kids, especially at night

## Teach these five rules

1. Never share your full name, school, address or phone number online
2. People online aren't always who they say they are
3. Never send photos to someone you've only met online
4. Never agree to meet an online friend without a parent
5. If anything makes you uncomfortable, tell a trusted adult. **You won't be in trouble.**

## Grooming warning signs

Groomers often build trust slowly. Watch for:

- A new "friend" who gives lots of compliments, gifts or game credits
- Requests to move the chat to a private app
- Asking the child to keep the friendship secret
- Sudden secrecy about devices, or distress after being online

If you suspect grooming: keep the evidence, don't confront the person yourself, report to the platform and the police.

## Gaming safety

- Turn off voice and text chat with strangers in game settings
- Require approval for in-game purchases
- Watch out for scams offering "free" game currency or skins in exchange for login details

## Keep talking

Ask open questions: *What's your favourite game right now? Has anyone online ever made you feel weird?* Kids who feel safe talking to you are the best-protected kids.

**2.** Name two tools that let parents manage apps and screen time.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Children who fear punishment or losing their device often hide problems. Reassurance keeps them reporting early.</details>
