Online shopping and digital wallets are convenient, and scammers know it. Most payment scams rely on the same few tricks. Once you know them, they're much easier to spot.

## Scam 1: The fake online shop

A Facebook or Instagram page sells branded shoes, phones or clothes at **50–80% off**. You pay in advance and nothing arrives, or you get something completely different.

**Warning signs**

- Prices far below what everyone else charges
- New page, few real reviews, comments turned off
- Only accepts advance payment to a personal account or wallet
- Pressure: "Only 2 left, offer ends tonight"

## Scam 2: "I sent you money by mistake"

You get a message (sometimes with a fake screenshot) saying someone accidentally sent you money and asking you to send it back. **No money ever arrived.** The screenshot is edited.

**Rule:** Always check your actual balance in the official app. Never rely on screenshots or SMS alone.

## Scam 3: Fake payment request

When you sell something online, the "buyer" sends a link or QR code to "receive" your payment. Scanning it actually makes **you pay them**.

**Rule:** You never need to scan a QR code or enter a PIN to *receive* money.

## Scam 4: Fake customer support

You search for a bank or wallet helpline and call a number from an ad or a random website. The "agent" asks you to install a screen-sharing app or share an OTP.

**Rule:** Get support numbers only from the official app or website, and never install remote-access apps for a stranger.

## Safe shopping checklist

- Buy from known sellers or platforms with buyer protection
- Prefer **cash on delivery** with new or unknown sellers
- Check the page's history, reviews and contact details
- Never share **OTP, PIN or CVV** with anyone
- Turn on transaction alerts for your bank and wallet

## If you've been scammed

1. Contact your bank or wallet provider **immediately** and ask them to block or dispute the transaction
2. Change PINs and passwords if you shared them
3. Save evidence: screenshots, profile links, transaction IDs
4. Report it to the police (in Nepal, the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau) and to the platform
5. Warn friends and family so they don't fall for the same page

## Exercises

**1.** A buyer sends you a QR code and says "Scan this to receive Rs. 5,000." What do you do?

**2.** You get an SMS saying "Rs. 2,000 credited to your wallet" and then a call asking you to return it. What's the first thing to check?

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Open the official wallet or bank app and check the real balance. Fake SMS and screenshots are common. If nothing arrived, block the number.</details>

**3.** List three warning signs of a fake online shop.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Any three of: unrealistically low prices, new page with few genuine reviews, comments disabled, advance payment only to a personal account, urgency pressure, no physical address or contact details.</details>

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>You only pay after you see the product, so there's nothing to steal if the shop is fake.</details>
