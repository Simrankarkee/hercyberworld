Most hacked accounts aren't "hacked" in a movie way. Someone simply **guessed or reused** a password. Good news: that's the easiest thing to fix.

## 1. Use passphrases, not passwords

Instead of `Simran@123`, string together random words:

> `pink-mango-river-laptop`

It's long, easy to remember, and much harder to crack.

## 2. One account, one password

If one website leaks your password, attackers try it on **everything**: email, Facebook, banking. Use a different password for each important account.

A **password manager** (like Bitwarden, 1Password or the one built into your phone) remembers them all for you.

## 3. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA means that even if someone has your password, they also need a code from **your phone**.

Turn it on first for:

- your **email** (it can reset every other account!)
- Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp
- banking and wallet apps

Authenticator apps are safer than SMS codes, but any 2FA is better than none.

## 4. Never share your OTP

No bank, telecom, delivery company or "friend in trouble" needs your OTP. **Anyone asking for it is a scammer.**

## Your 5-minute action list

- [ ] Change your email password to a passphrase
- [ ] Turn on 2FA for email
- [ ] Turn on 2FA for your top social media app
- [ ] Check [haveibeenpwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com) to see if your email was in a data breach

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>Your email. It can be used to reset the passwords of almost every other account.</details>
