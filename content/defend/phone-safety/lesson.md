Your phone holds your banking apps, photos, messages and the codes that protect every other account. Protecting it is one of the highest-value things you can do.

## 1. Lock it properly

- Use a **6-digit PIN or longer**, plus fingerprint or face unlock
- Avoid patterns (they're easy to watch over your shoulder) and obvious PINs like `123456` or your birth year
- Set the screen to lock automatically after 30 seconds to 1 minute
- Hide message previews on the lock screen so OTPs aren't visible

## 2. Keep it updated

Updates fix security holes attackers actively use. Turn on **automatic updates** for the system and for apps. If your phone no longer receives security updates, it's time to plan an upgrade.

## 3. Only install apps from official stores

- Download from **Google Play** or the **App Store** only
- Never install **APK files** sent via WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS. "Wedding invitation.apk" and "Bank update.apk" are common malware tricks.
- Check the developer name, downloads and reviews before installing
- Keep **Google Play Protect** turned on (Android)

## 4. Review app permissions

Ask yourself: *does this app really need this?*

- A torch app does not need your contacts
- A game does not need your SMS messages
- Be very careful with **Accessibility** permissions on Android. Banking malware often abuses them to read your screen.

Check: Settings → Privacy (or Apps) → Permission manager.

## 5. Prepare for loss or theft

- Turn on **Find My Device** (Android) or **Find My** (iPhone)
- Back up photos and contacts
- Know how to remotely lock and erase your phone
- Put a SIM PIN on your SIM card so a thief can't receive your OTPs on another phone

## 6. Be careful on public Wi-Fi

Avoid logging in to banking on open café or airport Wi-Fi. Use mobile data instead, or a reputable VPN.
