"How do I get into cybersecurity?" is the most common question in this field. There's no single path, but there is a sensible order to build skills in.

## Step 1: Build the foundations

Before specialising, get comfortable with:

- **Networking**: IP addressing, TCP/UDP, DNS, HTTP, firewalls
- **Operating systems**: Linux command line and Windows administration
- **Security concepts**: CIA triad, authentication, common attacks
- **Basic scripting**: Python or Bash to automate small tasks

## Step 2: Choose a direction

| Path | What you do | Typical first role |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue team (defence)** | Monitor alerts, investigate incidents | SOC Analyst (Tier 1) |
| **Red team (offence)** | Test systems by attacking them legally | Junior Penetration Tester |
| **GRC** | Governance, risk, compliance, policies and audits | GRC / Risk Analyst |
| **Cloud security** | Secure AWS, Azure or GCP environments | Cloud Security Associate |
| **Application security** | Find and fix flaws in software | AppSec / Security Engineer |
| **Digital forensics** | Investigate what happened after an incident | Forensics / IR Analyst |

Many people start in IT support, networking or a SOC and move across later.

## Step 3: Consider certifications

Certifications help you get past CV filters, but **skills and projects matter more**.

| Level | Certification | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | **ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC)** | Absolute beginners |
| Entry | **CompTIA Security+** | Widely requested baseline for many roles |
| Entry (practical) | **eJPT** (INE) | Hands-on intro to penetration testing |
| Intermediate | **CySA+**, **BTL1** | Blue team and SOC skills |
| Advanced | **OSCP** | Respected hands-on penetration testing cert |

Check each provider's current pricing and any free or discounted programmes before you book.

## Step 4: Prove your skills

- Complete labs on **TryHackMe**, **Hack The Box** or **LetsDefend**
- Play **CTFs** and publish your write-ups
- Build a **home lab** with virtual machines
- Write about what you learn (a blog like this one counts)
- Put projects on **GitHub** and keep **LinkedIn** active

> You don't need to know everything. You need to show you can learn, explain your thinking and keep going.
